# Build an AI Business From Scratch — Companion Workbook

**Status:** Draft course-support material, not proof of a finished video course or a validated business outcome.

This companion follows the five live-text lesson outlines. Use only fictional or approved information when testing AI tools. Avoid passwords, confidential customer data, copyrighted training material without permission, or unverified income promises.

## Starting checklist

- I know whose problem I am trying to solve.
- I have not confused generated market opinions with actual customer interviews.
- I will check every public claim, URL, price, and action before publishing.
- I have a manual fallback for automations and real-world consequences.

---

## Part 1: Choose the idea

**Outcome:** A one-sentence hypothesis plus evidence from potential customers.

### Do the work
1. List three recurring pains you have direct experience with.
2. Score urgency, recurrence, ability to reach buyers, and deliverability on a 1–5 scale; do not treat this score as proof of demand.
3. Use this offer template: We help [specific people] achieve [specific result] without [current frustration].
4. Interview five relevant people about their most recent experience; record exact answers without leading them.
5. Decide continue / revise / stop and state what evidence changed your mind.

### Evidence log
- Inputs or source facts: ______
- Completed result: ______
- Real-world validation or test: ______
- What I changed after checking: ______
- What remains unknown: ______

### Knowledge check
Which is stronger: a high AI-generated demand score, or repeated real accounts of the problem from likely customers?

**Instructor answer:** Real evidence from relevant customers is the stronger signal; customer statements still do not guarantee willingness to pay.

### Short instructional video script (draft only, not filmed)
Opening: 'An idea is a hypothesis.' Demo: score three fictional ideas and show how interviews change the ranking. Close: 'Do not build until you can say what you learned.'

## Part 2: Build the brand and site

**Outcome:** A clear business promise and a usable first website plan.

### Do the work
1. Write a brand statement without jargon or claims you cannot support.
2. Sketch a hero, evidence/explanation, offer, one CTA, and a readable footer.
3. Write the headline in under 15 words and have one unfamiliar person explain what the offer means.
4. Create a responsive first page in your chosen builder; verify links, forms, contact path and accessibility labels.
5. Record actual desktop and phone results, missing evidence, and fixes.

### Evidence log
- Inputs or source facts: ______
- Completed result: ______
- Real-world validation or test: ______
- What I changed after checking: ______
- What remains unknown: ______

### Knowledge check
Is a pretty mockup with a dead CTA a completed business website?

**Instructor answer:** No. A user must actually be able to navigate and complete the intended action.

### Short instructional video script (draft only, not filmed)
Opening: 'Your homepage has one job.' Demo: replace a vague hero with customer/result/CTA and click the sample route. Close: 'Test the phone experience before celebrating.'

## Part 3: Create the content engine

**Outcome:** A week of useful platform-adapted content linked to the offer.

### Do the work
1. Choose three pillars: Teach, Show, Invite.
2. Write ten customer questions grounded in actual observations or label guesses as hypotheses.
3. Pick one question and outline a 30-second explanation, a written post, and a carousel.
4. Add a CTA only when the content earns it; link to a real resource.
5. Plan one week and record published outcomes separately from drafts.

### Evidence log
- Inputs or source facts: ______
- Completed result: ______
- Real-world validation or test: ______
- What I changed after checking: ______
- What remains unknown: ______

### Knowledge check
Is posting ten AI-generated pieces the same thing as demonstrating real customer interest?

**Instructor answer:** No. Content volume is an output; qualified response and conversion need their own evidence.

### Short instructional video script (draft only, not filmed)
Opening: 'Publish something that helps.' Demo: turn one question into three channel-appropriate examples. Close: 'Track useful replies, not just output count.'

## Part 4: Automate the workflow

**Outcome:** A bounded workflow with validation, human approval, and an event log.

### Do the work
1. Map a process you have done manually at least twice.
2. Write each stage: trigger → validate → AI step → reviewer approval → action → log.
3. Use synthetic data and test normal, missing, duplicate, and malicious-looking inputs.
4. Verify that a rejected or unapproved record cannot cause an external send or write.
5. Write a manual fallback and rollback plan.

### Evidence log
- Inputs or source facts: ______
- Completed result: ______
- Real-world validation or test: ______
- What I changed after checking: ______
- What remains unknown: ______

### Knowledge check
If a model-generated response looks correct, should it trigger a production payment or customer email automatically?

**Instructor answer:** Not without the specific authorization and tested approval controls required for that action.

### Short instructional video script (draft only, not filmed)
Opening: 'Automate repetition, not accountability.' Demo: show a draft queue blocking an unapproved email. Close: 'Keep the log and manual escape hatch.'

## Part 5: Turn it into income

**Outcome:** A simple paid-offer hypothesis and a 30-day validation plan.

### Do the work
1. State the customer problem, concrete deliverable, and exclusions.
2. Calculate a tentative price range from delivery cost and value; mark untested assumptions.
3. Draft one honest offer and do not imply guaranteed outcomes.
4. Speak with ten relevant prospects through permissioned outreach; track decisions and objections.
5. At day 30, review conversations, trials, customers, revenue, and time-to-deliver; decide continue / refine / stop.

### Evidence log
- Inputs or source facts: ______
- Completed result: ______
- Real-world validation or test: ______
- What I changed after checking: ______
- What remains unknown: ______

### Knowledge check
Does a published checkout URL establish that buyers have purchased or received the offer?

**Instructor answer:** No. Verify real authorized transactions and fulfillment separately; never create test charges on production without permission.

### Short instructional video script (draft only, not filmed)
Opening: 'Charge for the result, not the model.' Demo: compare an AI buzzword offer with a specific deliverable and acceptance checklist. Close: 'Let customer evidence determine the next build.'

## Capstone: Evidence-backed small business system

Deliver: (1) one-sentence offer hypothesis; (2) actual customer-learning notes; (3) a usable first-page test; (4) one-week content plan; (5) a gated automation map and test evidence; (6) a 30-day offer test with honest metrics.

**Rubric — 0 not shown / 1 partly shown / 2 demonstrated, each:** customer evidence, offer clarity, tested website path, content usefulness, safety of automation, and measured learning. Maximum 12 points. A passing workbook is educational evidence, not proof that a real business is profitable.
