# AI Basics for Everyday Work — Student Workbook

Use AI safely for writing, research, planning, and everyday decisions without confusing fluent output with verified truth.

Estimated instructional time: 125 minutes. These are practice estimates, not a guaranteed duration.

**Completion outcome:** A reusable, human-reviewed five-step work routine with real deliverables.

**Privacy rule:** Use fictional or permissioned materials. Do not paste credentials, sensitive personal records, or confidential customer information.


---
## Part 1: Understand AI without the hype

**You should be able to:** Explain the difference between useful drafting and verified knowledge.

### Working notes
- **Understand the principle:** A language model predicts useful language from patterns. It can organize, draft, classify, and transform material, but convincing wording is not evidence that a statement is true. For factual or high-stakes decisions, compare the response with trustworthy original sources.
- **Use the method:** Sort tasks into low-risk drafts (ideas, outlines, formatting) and verification-required outputs (dates, prices, legal/health claims, statistics, citations). Remove private identifiers unless a specifically authorized tool and purpose require them.

### Compare the prompts
**Weak:** Tell me everything about my customer and guarantee this is correct.

**Improved:** Using only the fictional case below, write three possible ways to explain our product. Label assumptions, do not invent customer testimony, and list facts I must verify.

**Verify:** The response contains no invented testimonials, promises, or unsourced factual claims.

### Your practice
1. Pick a task you do twice a week and mark what AI may draft versus what you must check.
2. Give an AI tool a fictional example; identify at least three plausible-sounding unsupported statements.
3. Create a red/yellow/green risk checklist for your task.

## Part 2: Give useful instructions

**You should be able to:** Write a clear prompt with goal, audience, inputs, constraints, and output format.

### Working notes
- **Understand the principle:** 'Write an email' omits the recipient, purpose, relevant facts, tone, and length. Better instructions reduce revisions but cannot force truth. The most important part is the real outcome: what the human needs to do next.
- **Use the method:** Use GOAL → AUDIENCE → CONTEXT → RULES → FORMAT → CHECK. Supply only facts you can share. Ask the model to call out missing information rather than quietly invent it.

### Compare the prompts
**Weak:** Write an email about the meeting.

**Improved:** Draft a 120-word friendly email to our volunteer team. Goal: confirm Tuesday at 4 PM (time provided as example). Use only the attached agenda summary; state that the location is not yet confirmed. End by asking for accessibility needs.

**Verify:** The email uses the specified fictional time and leaves location unresolved instead of making one up.

### Your practice
1. Rewrite one vague instruction using all six prompt fields.
2. Test the original and improved prompt with the same fictional data.
3. Record two differences and one remaining human check.

## Part 3: Edit writing rather than surrendering it

**You should be able to:** Produce and revise a clear message while preserving your own meaning.

### Working notes
- **Understand the principle:** AI drafts are proposals. The owner of the message remains responsible for facts, promises, tone, and recipients. A polished message that changes the decision is a failure.
- **Use the method:** First ask for a draft. Then ask for a change log: what was condensed, reordered, or inferred. Compare against your original facts; restore anything misrepresented. Keep private contact details out of exercises.

### Compare the prompts
**Weak:** Make my cancellation request sound smart and say the company already agreed.

**Improved:** Rewrite this cancellation request so it is concise and firm. Facts: I requested cancellation on Monday; confirmation is pending. Do not claim approval or a refund. Mark anything that needs a date or account-specific check.

**Verify:** The revision does not invent cancellation approval, a refund, or an unprovided deadline.

### Your practice
1. Write a five-sentence message with two facts and one unresolved item.
2. Use AI to revise it in two distinct tones; inspect both against the original.
3. Choose one and annotate every factual assertion before sending.

## Part 4: Summarize sources without invented evidence

**You should be able to:** Separate a source's claims from your analysis and unknowns.

### Working notes
- **Understand the principle:** A summary is only as reliable as its input. Do not ask a model to 'find a citation' for an unsupported claim. Give it actual excerpts or source links when supported, and verify quotations, numbers, dates, and context in the original.
- **Use the method:** Use a two-column notes table: What the source actually says; What I infer. Ask for the key claim, supporting excerpt, date, and a question that remains unresolved. Two sources repeating one press release are not independent evidence.

### Compare the prompts
**Weak:** Prove the new tool is the most accurate on the market.

**Improved:** Summarize these two supplied product notes. For each, list exact stated capabilities, limits, and what cannot be compared because neither note gives benchmark methods. Do not invent test scores or customer feedback.

**Verify:** Every conclusion can be traced to the supplied material; unsupported comparisons are labeled unknown.

### Your practice
1. Find two public primary sources about a low-stakes topic; record publication dates.
2. Draft a source-grounded summary and check five sentences manually.
3. Circle one assumption and write a follow-up evidence question.

## Part 5: Build your personal AI work routine

**You should be able to:** Finish a small weekly workflow with a result, checks, and a fallback.

### Working notes
- **Understand the principle:** The useful endpoint is a finished result, not a chat transcript. Plan from the deliverable backward: what is due, what information is available, what depends on someone else, and what could go wrong.
- **Use the method:** Complete one capstone: take a non-sensitive task such as a club meeting plan or fictional event, draft the materials, compare against the source facts, and prepare a checklist. Keep the final approval and sending with a person.

### Compare the prompts
**Weak:** Make a perfect event plan and automatically send it.

**Improved:** Create a one-page plan for a fictional community workshop using the supplied venue and date. Identify missing budget and accessibility details. Draft an agenda, task owners as roles not names, verification checklist, and manual fallback.

**Verify:** The plan separates provided details from unknowns and includes a clear owner check before publication.

### Your practice
1. Choose one workflow and state a measurable result.
2. Use AI to draft the output, then do a source and privacy review.
3. Save a reusable prompt, a reviewed example, and a three-line 'what I would change next time' note.
